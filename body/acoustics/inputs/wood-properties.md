# Supercaster II wood properties: inputs for the acoustics models

2026-11-12. The machine-readable copy is `wood-properties.json`, which has every value with its range, unit, tag and source. It was generated by `build_wood_json.py` (stdlib only; run `python3 -I build_wood_json.py wood-properties.json`), and the checks in §6 came from that run.

Tags:
- **[literature: source]** a number taken from a source I opened.
- **[computed]** arithmetic on tagged inputs.
- **[judged]** my estimate where no direct data exists. The reason is given each time.

Nothing here has been measured on Jordan's boards yet. The tap test in `tap-test-procedure.md` replaces the most important rows (E_L, E across the grain, density, η, moisture content) with measured values.

## 1. Conventions

- **Axes.** L runs along the grain, R is radial (across the growth rings) and T is tangential (along the rings).
- **Moduli** are dynamic (audio-frequency) and free of shear deflection. That is what a free-free tap measures. The Wood Handbook's bending MOE includes shear deflection, so E_L = 1.1 × the table value (handbook note to Table 5-1).
- **Poisson's ratios.** ν_ij is the contraction along j under stress along i (handbook convention).
  - The major ratios ν_LR, ν_LT and ν_RT are the inputs.
  - The minor ratios come from symmetry: ν_ji = ν_ij·E_j/E_i. Measured minor ratios rarely satisfy this, so the model should use the symmetric ones (§6).
- **Damping** is the loss factor η (= tan δ).
  - Logarithmic decrement δ = π·η.
  - Q = 1/η.
  - T60 = 2.2/(f·η) for one mode.
- **Ranges** are plausible bounds covering roughly the central 80–90% (about ±1.5σ for the handbook coefficients of variation), not ±2σ. For roasted wood, the scatter of the unroasted wood and the uncertainty in the roasting factor are combined in log space. For a Monte Carlo, sample log-normally with σ = ln(high/central)/1.5 above and ln(central/low)/1.5 below.
- **Basis.** All values are at 12% moisture content (about 65% RH at 20 °C) unless stated otherwise. Roasted values are at that same climate, so they already include the lower equilibrium moisture of roasted wood (§5).

Where each number comes from:

**Red alder, unroasted**
- Density: SG 0.41 from Table 5-3a, × 1.12 for the 12% moisture-content basis [computed]. The Wood Database gives 450 [literature].
- E_L: 9.5 GPa × 1.1 [computed from FPL-GTR-282 Table 5-3a].
- MOR 68 MPa, tension perpendicular 2.9 MPa, side hardness 2.6 kN [literature: Table 5-3a].
- Ranges come from the Table 5-6 coefficients of variation: MOE 22%, SG 10%, MOR 16%, tension perpendicular 25% [literature].
- **Red alder is not in the handbook's elastic-constant tables (5-1, 5-2).** The ratios E_T/E_L 0.045, E_R/E_L 0.088, G_LR/E_L 0.074, G_LT/E_L 0.059 and G_RT/E_L 0.016, and the Poisson's ratios, are the mean of the diffuse-porous hardwoods of similar density in Tables 5-1 and 5-2: yellow-poplar, cottonwood, basswood, sweetgum and yellow birch [judged]. Brémaud et al. (2011) find that hardwood anisotropy rises as density falls, which supports using low-density proxies rather than the "standard hardwood" set.
- **Damping.** η_L = 8.0×10⁻³ is the hardwood median in Brémaud et al. (2011), Table 4: 8.1×10⁻³ at E/ρ 20.5 GPa. Alder's E/ρ is about 22.8 [literature, applied by judgement].
  - That paper's table header reads "(%)". The values only make sense ×10⁻³, which matches the 3.3–20.6×10⁻³ range over 98 woods.
  - Cross-grain and shear η use the median ratios from Brémaud et al. (2011) [computed]:
    - tan δ_R/tan δ_L = 2.47 (39 hardwoods, range 1.49–4.12)
    - tan δ_T/tan δ_R = 1.14
    - tan δ_shear/tan δ_L ≈ 2.2
  - I found no published damping measurement for alder.
- **K_Ic** is judged. Alder is not in Table 5-10; yellow-poplar is 517, sugar maple 480 and the softwoods 250–440 kPa·m^½.

**Hard maple (Acer saccharum), unroasted**
- From Tables 5-1, 5-2 and 5-3a: SG 0.63, MOE 12.6 GPa, MOR 109 MPa, elastic ratios 0.065 / 0.132 / 0.111 / 0.063, Poisson's ratios 0.424 / 0.476 / 0.774 / 0.349 [literature].
- G_RT is not given; I used 0.020·E_L [judged].
- Tension perpendicular is blank for sugar maple in Table 5-3a. Black maple's 4.6 MPa stands in [judged].
- K_Ic 480 kPa·m^½ [literature: Table 5-10].
- η_L 8.5×10⁻³ [judged]:
  - It follows the Brémaud median at maple's E/ρ of 19.7.
  - Danihelová et al. (2022) measured 17.5×10⁻³ on sycamore maple, curly pieces included. That is about 2× the Brémaud median, and the gap is unexplained. Possible causes: they measured the ~5 kHz longitudinal mode, the species is sycamore not hard maple, curly pieces were included, and the rig adds loss. So it sets the top of the range, not the centre.

**East Indian rosewood (Dalbergia latifolia)**
- ρ 855 kg/m³, E 12.30 GPa, MOR 115 MPa [literature: Wood Database]. Table 5-5a gives 12.3 GPa and 116.5 MPa at 12% [literature].
- The handbook's SG of 0.75 is on a green-volume basis. Converted with 8.6% volumetric shrinkage it gives about 880 kg/m³ [computed], which agrees.
- Elastic ratios are Brémaud's "standard hardwood" set at SG 0.65 [literature, applied as a proxy].
- Poisson's ratios are the mean of the handbook hardwoods with SG ≥ 0.5 [judged].
- η_L 6×10⁻³ [judged]. Brémaud et al. (2008) list rosewoods among the tropical woods with damping below the usual trend against E/ρ. I could not read a *D. latifolia* number in the sources I opened.

**Maple veneer, 0.6 mm**
- Same as unroasted hard maple along its grain.
- For a membrane or shell model, the in-plane constants are E_L (along the veneer), E_T (across it, in plane), G_LT and ν_LT. E_R and G_LR are through-thickness.
- E across the veneer is cut to 0.8× (0.55–1.0) for possible lathe checks, if the veneer is rotary-cut [judged].
- The 3-D model used 12 GPa along the veneer grain and 0.8 GPa across it (`final/liner-RESULT.md` §2). That sits inside these ranges.
- **Thickness tolerance matters.** The veneer works as a membrane under the roof, so its stiffness contribution scales with t. A ±0.05 mm spread is ±8%.

## 3. The roasting adjustments

No source gives roasted red alder or roasted *Acer saccharum*. So I took **ratios** (treated ÷ untreated, same climate) from studies of related species and applied them to the handbook baseline for the right species. The studies:

**Alder**
- *Alnus glutinosa*, Borůvka et al. 2015, at 165 °C and 210 °C.
- *A. glutinosa*, Grinins et al. 2025, at 160–170 °C in nitrogen.
- *A. rubra*, Liu et al. 2022, at 140 °C only.

**Maple**
- *Acer pseudoplatanus*, Danihelová et al. 2022, at 135–185 °C.
- *A. pseudoplatanus*, Aydoğmuş 2025, at 210 °C for 90 min.

**Cross-grain**
- Beech and birch, Al-Musawi et al. 2023, at 150–250 °C.

The supplier's schedule is unknown, so each factor brackets the mild-to-severe range.

**Curly maple neck blank.** I applied a figure correction [judged] to the roasted maple: E_L ×0.85 (0.70–1.00), E_R and E_T ×1.05, η_L ×1.25 (1.0–1.6).
- Brémaud et al. (2011) note that curly maple has the lowest L-to-R anisotropy of any hardwood, because of its systematic grain deviation.
- A DMA study of wavy-grain sycamore reports high damping.
- **This is the least reliable row in the file.** The tap test should replace it.

## 4. Two results that matter for the build (no design change proposed)

**1. Cross-grain strength of the roasted top.**
- Roasting alone gives 2.9 × (0.45–0.85) = **1.3–2.5 MPa, central 1.9 MPa** [computed]. Adding board-to-board scatter widens it to 0.9–3.0 MPa.
- The 3-D study and its check assumed 2.0 MPa (`sim3d-REPORT.md`, `sim3d-CHECK.md` §7). That is the central value here.
- If the supplier roasted near 210 °C, the true value is more likely in the lower half.
- The offcut break test (plan §0, bench step 1) settles this directly. The plan already sets the top skin from that test.

**2. Cross-grain stiffness of the top depends on how the board was sawn.**
- In the plane of the top, E across the grain is [computed from the central constants]:
  - quartersawn (rings at 90° to the face): 0.92 GPa
  - 30° ring angle: 0.64 GPa
  - rift (45°): 0.53 GPa
  - flatsawn: 0.47 GPa
- Rift is close to flatsawn because G_RT is small.
- The models so far used 0.67 GPa. That is 1.3–1.4× the flatsawn/rift central value and 0.7× the quartersawn value.
- `sim3d-CHECK.md` found that wood stiffness barely moves the gain once the liner is re-tuned. The dry-clamp air-resonance reading and throat trim already absorb this [judged].
- The value still matters for the wing T1 mode and the top's strength margin. An end-grain photo of the blank picks the row; the tap test measures it.

## 5. Moisture

- Handbook rule (ch. 5): P(M) = P12·(P12/P_green)^((12−M)/(Mp−12)), with Mp = 25% when not listed [literature].
- From 12% to 8% moisture content, unroasted [computed]:
  - alder: E_L ×1.05, MOR ×1.14, tension perpendicular ×1.02
  - maple: E_L ×1.05, MOR ×1.17
- Density falls about 0.45% for each 1% of moisture lost (alder) [computed].
- Internal friction is lowest near 6% moisture content at 23 °C and rises on either side [literature: handbook ch. 5].
- **Build climate.** Assumed 40–45% RH. Unroasted wood sits near 8% and roasted near 4.5–5.5% [judged from the equilibrium data in §3].
- Pin moisture meters are not calibrated for roasted wood [judged]. Use oven-dry or stable-weight readings (see the procedure).

## 6. Self-checks [computed]

- **Positive definiteness.** The 6×6 compliance matrix is positive-definite for every material at the central values. It stays so at the worst corner: high Poisson's ratios with low E_R and E_T.
- **Minor Poisson's ratios.** These come from symmetry, and the model should use them. The proxy-measured minors in the JSON differ from them by up to 25%, which is normal for wood.

  | | ν_RL | ν_TL | ν_TR |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Alder | 0.031 | 0.019 | 0.395 |
  | Maple | 0.056 | 0.031 | 0.381 |
  | Rosewood | 0.048 | 0.036 | 0.380 |

- **Plausibility.** The expected free-free frequencies for the tap-test strips (in the procedure) fall at 240–350 Hz in bending and 4.8–6.4 kHz in the longitudinal mode, which is easy to record on a phone.

## 7. Numbers that would help most from Jordan's published ranges

Ranked by how much each one changes a decision or a model output:

1. **The supplier's roasting schedule for the alder and for the maple neck blank:** peak temperature, hold time, atmosphere (steam, nitrogen or air) and mass loss %. This picks the mild or severe end of every factor in §3. It is the single biggest lever on the strength numbers.
2. **The cross-grain strength of roasted alder,** or any roasted diffuse-porous hardwood (birch, poplar, alder) at a known schedule. Tension perpendicular to the grain is best; cross-grain MOR or a 3-point bend across the grain is next. This sets the top-skin margin.
3. **E_R and E_T (or E across the grain) of roasted alder or birch.** These set the wing T1 mode and the veneer's share of the roof stiffness.
4. **A full elastic-constant set for unroasted red alder from one source:** E_R, E_T, the three G values and the Poisson's ratios. Bucur, Guitard and Kretschmann are likely places. It replaces the proxy ratios.
5. **η_L and η_R for red alder,** unroasted and roasted, at 100–1000 Hz. These set the wing and air-resonance Q.
6. **E_L and η_L for curly or flame hard maple,** ideally roasted and quartersawn. This affects the neck mode placement and dead spots.
7. **Mode-I fracture toughness or cleavage strength of roasted alder.** This matters for cracking at the f-hole tips and at screw pilots.
8. **East Indian rosewood η_L** (minor).
9. **About the boards Jordan has** (not literature):
   - weight and dimensions of the alder blank and the neck blank
   - an end-grain photo of each, to see the ring angle (§4)
   - the veneer's species and cut (rotary or sliced) and whether it is roasted
   - whether the fretboard is roasted
