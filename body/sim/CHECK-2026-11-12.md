## 1. Model build: partly holds

**What holds** [computed, by reading the code and hand checks]

- **Units:** SI throughout. Inch geometry is converted once (IN = 0.0254).
- **Orthotropy:**
  - Alder is 'LTR': L runs along the neck axis x, T across, R through the thickness. That is right for flat-sawn stock.
  - The maple tenon is 'LRT'. The veneer is cross-grain 'TLR'.
  - `wood_D` keeps the reciprocity ν_ij/E_i = ν_ji/E_j.
- **Element:** the QM6 incompatible-mode hex with the Taylor correction is implemented correctly.
- **Air coupling:**
  - W is the volume decrease, so the pressure is physical. Port flow is outward, and port and wall move in antiphase below A0, as they should.
  - The A0 eigenmatrix `[[Λ, Cpᵀ], [CpΛ, CpCpᵀ+Ω]]` is consistent with (Λ−ω²)a + Cpᵀb = f and (Ω−ω²)b + ω²Cp a = 0.
- **BEM:**
  - Conventions: CBIE `p/2 − ∫p ∂G/∂n = iωρ∫G v_n`, outward n, e^{iωt}, G = e^{−ikr}/4πr.
  - The signs and the self term are correct. Using the Hermitian part for the radiation resistance is valid by reciprocity.
- **T60:** the formula T60 = 6.91 L/(T G) is correct.
- **Trem routes** in `model.py` match the plan notes (see section 5).

**What is wrong or weak**

1. **Rigid-mode bug in the free-body runs** [computed]
   - The dedupe line in `solve_modes` treats every λ ≤ 0 as equal, so it collapses rigid modes:
     `keep = np.r_[True, np.diff(np.sqrt(np.clip(lam, 0, None))) > ...]`
   - The free run keeps only 4 modes below 10 Hz instead of 6. The cached λ are −1.65e-5, 1.1e-2, 96.2 and 926.
   - Springs whose two ends are not at the same point give the rigid rotations a spurious stiffness (pivot springs, and the claw-to-body springs at a different z).
   - Result: `final_narrowed_free` has 34.9 dB at 80–200 Hz against 45.97 dB held [computed from the npz].
   - Free 80–200 Hz results are invalid because of this bug. The true free-against-held difference is unknown, since some real difference is expected without the player contacts.
   - The held runs are not affected: their 6 support modes sit at 12.5–41 Hz. All of the STUDIES use held runs.
   - **Do not use any "free" 80–200 Hz number.**
2. **No prestress (geometric) stiffness anywhere** [computed, by code reading]
   - For the floating trem this matters. By hand, string tension (460.7 N) and spring pull (175 N) acting on lever arms of 0.25 in and 0.85 in give about −5.9 N·m/rad.
   - Against about 44 N·m/rad of elastic stiffness, that lowers the trem rocking frequency by about 7 % (see 3b).
3. **Neck joint** [judged]
   - The RBE2 ties only 0.6 in of body nodes (levels 0–3, |y| ≤ 1) to the beam end.
   - The neck over the body (x 15.69–18.69) contributes 23 g of lumped mass and no stiffness.
   - This is the leading untested suspect for the twist mode landing at 317–320 Hz against 287.9 Hz.
4. **Unexplained beam mass** [computed, by code reading; the interpretation is judged]
   - `model.py` line 380 has `s['rhoA'] + 0.14 * (bx >= 0) + 0.05`, with no comment. That is about 63 g plus about 33 g along the neck.
   - 0.14 kg/m equals a 3/16 in steel rod. If it is a rod proxy:
     - MODEL.md's "no truss rod" is wrong;
     - every `truss_rod` option counts the rod twice (about 60 g).
   - Rod-to-rod differences survive. Absolute mass, balance and neck-mode frequencies are biased.
   - Fix: document the term, or remove it when `truss_rod` is set.
5. **Damping** [judged]
   - eta_L is applied to all strain energy.
   - Shear and cross-grain loss in wood is typically higher than eta_L. Loss is therefore likely underestimated for shear-dominated plate and twist modes, which overstates their peak heights.
6. **Small items** [computed]
   - The slot void's own second moment is not subtracted from the neck section (about 2 % stiffness overstatement).
   - The tuner CoM is at z = −0.1 in (the plate midplane), but the comment says "0.4 in above the face".
   - The model's bosses are not the CAD's: 3 bosses filling 19 mm (liner depth) only where the screw centre is inside the chamber. The CAD has 4 bosses to z = −0.65 (about 11 mm below the roof).
7. **Air network** [computed]
   - My rough hand Helmholtz estimate for the bare hole is about 380–450 Hz, against the model's rigid-wall 379 Hz. This is plausible, but too coarse to confirm anything independently.
   - A0 runs 1.2–2.5 % below the reference in all four configurations. That is systematic, not scatter.

## 2. Validation: partly holds

**The reference numbers are genuine** [computed, by grep]

- Every REF value in `validate.py` appears verbatim in the earlier reports:
  - A0 285.8 / 325.7 / 377.1 / 319.6 Hz and the four band rows come from `acoustics/final/liner-CHECK.md`, nominal table (lines 120–123), with the 200–300 / 300–500 / 500–1000 / 80–200 columns mapped correctly.
  - Solid minus current −2.2 / −3.6 / −8.7 / −2.5 dB, 2.938 kg and 15.51 in come from `sim3d-REPORT.md`.
  - The twist mode at 287.9 Hz (held, the study's contacts) comes from `sim3d-CHECK.md`, line 36.
- The tolerances are in code.

**No sign of tuning** [judged]

- The failures are reported, not hidden: 18 of 28 checks pass.
  - A0 narrowed is −2.5 %.
  - 300–500 Hz is off by −6.5 to +2.7 dB.
  - Narrowed minus plain at 200–300 Hz is +2.5 against +3.8 dB.
  - The twist mode is 29 Hz high.
- No parameter in `designs.py` looks fitted to a reference value.

**Why this is not a validation** [judged]

- **No measurement.** The reference is the earlier model, and both models share the judged inputs: player contacts, pivot_k, damping and guard contact.
- **The reference geometry differs.** The earlier model's spring cavity started at x = 20.4 and cut into the tongue (`sim3d-REPORT.md` §6.4). The current model starts it further back. Part of the mismatch may therefore be error in the reference, not in the new model.
- **The narrowed-minus-plain shortfall is expected, not alarming.** `liner-CHECK.md` already showed that this gain depends on the twist mode meeting A0: with wood ×0.8 the gain falls from +3.8 to +1.1 dB. The new model's twist mode is 29 Hz higher, so a smaller gain follows.
- Read the model as "consistent with the old model where both are well posed", not as validated.

## 3. Studies

### 3a. Chamber against solid, and the room study: holds for power and room, partly at the ear

- **Room power is consistent** [computed]. It matches run_case per frequency within 0.04 dB. On the 0.5 Hz grid, final minus solid is:

  | Band | 80–200 | 200–300 | 300–500 | 500–1000 | Broadband |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Final − solid (dB) | +2.26 | +6.85 | +2.62 | +3.08 | +3.07 |

  At 2–3 m the room gives +3.07 / +3.14 dB.
- **The +6.8 dB at 200–300 Hz is mostly one peak** [computed]
  - The final build's A0 peak near 280 Hz (about C#4–D4) is 64.4 dB, against 50.8 dB for the solid body at the same frequency. That is about +13.5 dB at the peak.
  - At 250 Hz the gain is only about +3.5 dB.
  - Read it as "a strong resonance near C#4 plus about 3 dB elsewhere", not a uniform 6.8 dB lift.
- **The `final_narrowed` / `solid` result pair** [computed, from the npz]. Both records use the reference wood set. They give +2.20 / +6.02 / +2.43 / +2.68 dB, which is the same picture. This is not the room table's pair: no result file matches its 49.2 dB solid.
- **Band sampling** [computed]
  - The ear-grid run samples only 22 / 9 / 12 / 17 frequencies per band.
  - I offset a grid of the same density across the 0.5 Hz power spectra of the `final_narrowed` / `solid` pair. The band delta moves by ±0.01 dB at 80–200 Hz, ±0.3 dB at 200–300, ±0.45 dB at 300–500 and ±0.5 dB at 500–1000.
  - The room table's +2.8 / +3.2 dB (300–500 and broadband), against my full-grid +2.62 / +3.07 dB, is within this bound. The difference comes from sparse sampling, not from an error.
  - Ear spectra are peakier than power, so the ear-grid error is likely larger [judged].
- **Ear deltas** [judged]
  - At the ear, +9.5 to +25.6 dB at 200–300 Hz measures how deep the solid body's dipole null is. The document says so itself.
  - Use power and the room at 2–3 m.
- **Critical distance** [computed]
  - The standard formula r_c = 0.057·√(V/RT60) with Q = 1 [literature: standard Sabine critical-distance formula, e.g. Kuttruff, Room Acoustics] gives 0.57 m [computed], not "about 0.7 m".
  - The conclusion is unchanged: a listener at 2–3 m is reverberant-dominated.
  - The reverberant term also omits the (1−α) factor. That is a small, constant offset that cancels in the differences.

### 3b. Ti trem block: partly holds

**Hand model of the rocking mode** [computed]

- Rigid trem on the pivot, string axial springs (sum 1.84e5 N/m), and 3 springs.

| Case | I (kg·m²) | f, no prestress | f, with tension softening |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steel block, 3 springs | 2.811e-4 | 63.0 Hz | 58.6 Hz |
| Ti block, 3 springs | 1.766e-4 | 79.5 Hz | 73.9 Hz |
| Brass block, 3 springs | 3.01e-4 | 60.9 Hz | 56.6 Hz |
| Steel, 5 springs | | 66.7 Hz | 62.6 Hz |
| Ti, 5 springs | | 84.2 Hz | 79.0 Hz |

- **Stiffness:** K_θ is 35.9 N·m/rad from the strings and 8.14 from the springs. The spring share is 18 %, against the model's 18.6 %, so the elastic model agrees.

**The model's 66.3 Hz → 81.6 Hz shift is plausible, but**

- With prestress (about ×0.93), Ti lands near 76 Hz (D2–D#2), not on E2 (82.4 Hz). The "E2 +7.4 dB / F2 −6.5 dB" pattern will move by about a semitone.
- The model's steel block is 202 g (density × volume), but BALANCE.md uses 280 g (255–305 g). At 280 g the steel trem drops to about 51 Hz [computed]. The baseline note pattern then changes as well.
- I reproduced the model's E2 numbers from the JSON [computed]:
  - steel: conductance 3.36e-4 s/kg, body T60 143.4 s;
  - Ti: 1.44e-3 s/kg, 36.7 s.
- **What +7.4 dB means for decay** [computed]

  | String decay T_S | Steel T_eff | Ti T_eff | Change |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 8 s (the dead-spot study's value) | 7.58 s | 6.57 s | −13 % |
  | 15 s | | | −22 % |

  - The halving budget in `rom.py` (1.0e-3 to 3.0e-3 s/kg) implies T_S = 6.91 L/(T·G) ≈ 58 s on low E, falling to about 20 s on the trebles. This uses typical 10–46 tensions of 69–82 N per string [judged].
  - The two "dead" criteria in the project therefore differ by 2.5–7×, so "past halving" and "dead" counts are not comparable.

**Verdict.** "Keep the steel block" holds as a mechanism: a lighter block raises the rocking mode into the low-E range. The point numbers do not: E2, +7.4 dB and −17 cents.

## 4. Balance: holds (BALANCE.md)

**Neck-dive torque by hand** [computed]

- Inputs:
  - CoM (19.38, −0.39) in, and the horn button position from the plan file;
  - Δ = (6.34, −4.72) in, so d = 7.904 in = 0.2008 m;
  - m g d = 2.92 × 9.81 × 0.2008 = 5.75 N·m.
- Free-hang angle: 90° − atan(4.72/6.34) = 53.3°.
- Torque = m g d sin(θ_free − α):
  - α = 20°: 5.75 × sin 33.3° = **3.16 N·m**;
  - α = 30°: 5.75 × sin 23.3° = **2.27 N·m**.
  - Both match BALANCE.md.
- Headstock mass to dive at 30°: 547 g by hand, against 550 g reported.
- Seated: 0.493 N·m and 2.24 N forearm force, against 0.50 N·m and 2.3 N.

**Independent part sum** [computed]

- My own sum, with my own geometry and BALANCE's hardware masses, gives 2885 g, CoM x 19.31 in, y −0.39 in, and 53.0° free-hang.
- Torque 3.08 / 2.21 N·m at 20° / 30°, and 533 g to dive.
- This checks the geometry and the arithmetic, not the part list.

**The balance figures disagree across documents** [reported]

**Felt vibration** [computed]

## 5. CAD trem changes against the plan notes: partly holds

This section compared the tremolo routes in `final_carve.py` with dimensions measured from third-party 1:1 plan sheets
of a commercial guitar. The measured numbers and the plan coordinates are not published, so the section is cut down to
its findings. All results are [computed] from my parse of the plan vectors and from `final_carve.py`'s parameters.

- **Routes match the notes.** Slot, block cavity and spring cavity agree with the plan vectors, and `model.py` uses the same routes.
- **Centreline.** The routes are centred on the true centreline (the neck-screw midpoint). The block cavity is offset to the treble side, as modelled.
- **Through-slot.** The slot length comes from one stroke on the plan sheets; no second path confirms it. Confirm it against the physical trem (the block's string-through slot).
- **Slot hidden under the plate: fails, as already flagged.** The uncovered slot area is 0.176 in² by my raster, against 0.178 in² in CHANGES. The plate does not cover the slot's width. The CNC blocker stands.
- **Bosses.** 4 bosses qualify. Distances into the chamber are +0.750 / +0.075 / −0.100 / +0.100 in by my distance transform, against +0.775 / +0.075 / −0.075 / +0.100 in CHANGES. They agree within one raster cell.
- **Unreconciled, outside this change.** The plan's neck pocket ends 0.33 in short of the carve's pocket (pocket_datum 15.6875 + 3.0 = 18.6875), and its pocket is slightly wider than the carve's 2.1875 in. The glue-plane check is still false (a known item).
