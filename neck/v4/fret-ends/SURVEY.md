# Fret ends and fretboard edge: sourced survey

Date: 2026-11-14. Web research only; no design recommendations.

**Evidence grading.** [F] = I fetched the page and read it. [S] = the claim comes from a search-result summary and I could not open the page (several forums and the Warmoth blog returned 403/402). Quotes are as the source states them. "Not found" means I searched and found nothing. Most evidence is forum and shop opinion. Almost no factory publishes dimensions.

## 1. Standard bevelled ends

- StewMac sells a file that puts "a uniform 35° bevel" on fret ends [S] https://www.stewmac.com/shop-by-brand/stewmac/fret-beveling-file . Forum posters report 30-40°, and one says 45° "works well on regular to vintage sized frets" [S] https://www.tdpri.com/threads/fret-end-file-bevel-angle-question.289120/post-3543799 . Music Nomad's file uses 23° for finishing and 3° for initial filing [S] https://musicnomadcare.com/shop/guitar-fret-care/fret-beveling-file-b-file/ .
- Warmoth (neck maker) says it back-bevels "to about 30 degrees, and polish[es] them to a smooth finish" [S] https://warmoth.com/blog/warmoth-fret-installation . Owners report some Warmoth ends are sharp: "The fret ends were sharper than I prefer so I rounded them myself" [S] https://unofficialwarmoth.com/threads/do-these-fret-ends-look-fine-to-you.22597 .
- Hesh (Luthiers Forum, Dec 2024) uses a minimal bevel, "likely around 10 degrees", with the end taken "flush with the fret board edge". He calls a 45°+ bevel "too much" [F] https://luthiersforum.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=743372 .
- Finishing steps (StewMac, Erlewine): protect the finish with tape, flatten the overhang with the file's flat side, round with the smooth rounded side, polish with Micro-Mesh 6000-8000 then 12000, check by dragging a wooden dowel along the ends. No angles given [F] https://www.stewmac.com/video-and-ideas/trade-secrets/how-to-make-sharp-fret-ends-smooth-and-comfortable/ . StewMac's concave end files have a "30-degree angled end"; medium ".080" (2mm)" and wide ".120" (3mm)" sizes; used only "after the frets have been installed, trimmed flush to the sides, and beveled with a flat file" [F] https://www.stewmac.com/stewmac-essentials/concave-fret-end-files .
- Vintage Fender practice: **not found.** No source states original 1950s-60s Strat fret-end bevel or setback. Reissue listings give only "21 vintage tall frets" on a "7.25" radius" [S] https://www.fender.com/products/american-vintage-ii-1961-stratocaster-neck-21-vintage-tall-frets-725-radius-rosewood . Fender is said to have "installed frets sideways" [S] https://guitar.com/guides/essential-guide/vintage-fender-stratocaster-buying-guide/ .
- Lost fret length: a bevel takes some width at each end; no source gives a figure. Hesh's argument is that a flush end with a small bevel leaves "the most fret top surface" [F, p=743372].
- Stainless: Warmoth says dressing is the same as nickel "though Stainless Steel will take longer" [S, Warmoth blog]. StewMac's nipper guidance: "Take smaller bites than you would with nickel silver" [F] https://www.stewmac.com/video-and-ideas/online-resources/learn-about-guitar-and-instrument-fretting-and-fretwork/how-to-install-frets-bound-fingerboard/ . Philadelphia Luthier Tools calls Jescar FW43080-S "a lot harder and tougher to work with than nickel silver"; slot width recommended .023" [S] https://philadelphialuthiertools.com/fretwire/jescar-stainless-steel-fretwire-fw43080-s-electric-medium-pre-radiused . One of their files carries a "not for Stainless" note [S] https://www.philadelphialuthiertools.com/luthier-tools/files/fret-end-dressing-file-wide .

## 2. Hemispherical, ball, "semi-hemispherical" and dome ends

- **NK Forster Guitars** [F] https://nkforsterguitars.com/semi-hemispherical-fret-ends/ : a "bullet" grind with a diamond-coated wheel matched to the fretwire; wheel used about a year without visible wear; a machine shop is making 20 wheels for luthiers. Charges "quite a bit more" than standard (no figure). "it is shaping a fret end like a bullet, so it's very smooth to the touch"; gives "a fraction more fret 'real estate'". Needs "a lot more time" in shaping, polishing and fitting; frets are fitted so exactly that no levelling is needed afterwards. Works with nickel and 2 mm EVO Gold; **stainless untested**, "SS wrecks all cutting tools eventually" (this stainless quote is from the search summary [S]). Credits Harry Fleishman (1990s) with the idea. No radius or dimensions given.
- Method per a forum luthier: cut fret oversize, measure each slot with calipers, grind the end to size, nip tangs, file with a concave crowning file matched to the fret, polish, then install [S] https://www.mimf.com/phpbb/viewtopic.php?t=423 (via search summary). Shaping after installation is called "very difficult"; levelling afterwards leaves flat facets [S, same source family].
- Guitar Building Forum thread [F] https://luthiersforum.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=743372 : Dennis Kincheloe shapes before installing "so I can inset them a bit from the edges" and insets treble-side ends further. RNRoberts used a jig for semi-hemi ends with "very little vertical bevel on the fret end, leaves more useable acreage on the fret"; jig dimensions not on the page. SteveSmith shapes after installing. Chris Pile: "semi-hemispherical frets ends is just a luthier showing off." SteveSmith: after 4 or 5 such necks "none of the players noticed."
- Another builder uses a circular sander at angles; a grinding-jig user estimates it saves about an hour per job [S] https://luthiersforum.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=728374 . One shop: "no one has ever asked them for semi-hemi fret ends" [S] https://mandolincafe.com/forum/showthread.php?p=1845256 .
- Feel reports (forum, [S]): some find them smooth; others find the full quarter-sphere "gets in the way of the players hand" and "I could always feel the rounded frets too much"; a less-than-full roundover is said to feel about the same https://sevenstring.org/threads/how-do-you-feel-about-hemispherical-fret-ends.331664/ .
- Time and price: refret ranges $350-$600 for stainless including fret-end dressing [S] https://acousticvibesmusic.com/blogs/blog/cost-to-refret-a-guitar-2026-price-guide-and-luthier-insights . A hemispherical upcharge: **not found.** Named refret specialists offering it with published price: **not found.**
- Humidity/wear/refret/lost length: **not found** specific to hemispherical ends. The Forster and Kincheloe claims imply inset ends remove fret length at the edge; no number given.

## 3. Factory edge treatments

- **Ibanez Prestige / Premium "fret edge treatment":** Ibanez pages list "Prestige fret edge treatment" on jumbo stainless frets (e.g. https://www.ibanez.com/na/products/detail/rg5120m_00_01.html) [S]. Sweetwater says it rounds ends "much more precisely than is possible by hand filing" [S] https://www.sweetwater.com/store/detail/RG652AHMFXRPB--ibanez-prestige-rg652ahmfx-electric-guitar-royal-plum-burst . Andertons describes Premium as rounding and polishing the ends [S] https://www.andertons.co.uk/ibanez-guitars-guide . Method, dimensions and machine/hand: **not found**; one forum poster's guess that it is automated is speculation [S] https://strat-talk.com/threads/ibanez-premium-fret-end-treatment.532141/ . Owners report inconsistency: a Prestige with ends "snipped" and a 45° bevel but not rounded [S] https://sevenstring.org/threads/fret-ends-on-new-prestige-guitars.334677/page-2 .
- **Sire "Edgeless"** is rolled board edges on ebony; the fret-end claim rests on reviews [S] https://www.sire-usa.com/blogs/sire-blogs/edgeless-the-future-of-guitar-necks . No radius given.
- **Sadowsky:** a forum user relays a video saying they "hand finish rounded fret ends on masterbuilts" (second-hand) [S] https://www.talkbass.com/threads/help-sadowsky-me-took-a-dive.1677594/ ; CR Guitars lists rolled edges [S] https://www.crguitars.com/site/items/sadowsky-nyc-vintage-s-style .
- **Music Man:** a forum poster says a factory worker rounded board edges, probably with a sanding block (anecdote) [S] https://www.hamerfanclub.com/forums/topic/63618-rolled-fretboard-edges-how-to-do-it/ .
- **Suhr:** listings show "Standard Roll" rolled edges, 10"-14" radius, medium stainless frets [S] https://eddiesguitars.com/product/electric/electric-guitar-categories/solid-body-electric-guitars/suhr-standard-roasted-faded-trans-green-burst/ . Fret-end method: **not found.**
- **Fender:** a retailer lists the Limited American Professional II as "Roasted Maple, Rolled Edges", 9.5" radius, 22 narrow-tall frets [S] https://www.russomusic.com/products/fender-limited-american-professional-ii-stratocaster-electric-guitar-sunburst . Custom Shop Master Built copy: "rolled to perfection" (retailer) [S] https://themusicemporium.com/products/fender-masterbuilt-david-brown-60s-hss-stratocaster-black-journeyman . Fender's own definition of the roll and fret-end finish: **not found.** Premier Guitar calls a rolled board edge "a common custom-shop option" [F] https://www.premierguitar.com/diy/mod-garage/make-your-neck-feel-vintage .
- **Tom Anderson, Collings, PRS, Strandberg, Vigier, Mayones:** **not found** (no fret-end or edge spec located). Strandberg's own fret-sprout article could not be fetched.
- **Warmoth** (parts maker): "light rolled" only. Its hard finish makes "heavily rolled or rounded fretboard edges problematic on necks with dark fretboard woods" and players "can find that strings continually slip off the edge of the neck" [S, Warmoth blog].

## 4. Bound boards

- Gibson nibs: frets in first, ends filed flush, binding glued on, then scraped between frets leaving nibs over the ends [F] https://hazeguitars.com/blog/the-great-big-gibson-fret-nib-debate ; StewMac: "slapped on the binding then scraped the heck out of it between the frets" [S] https://www.stewmac.com/video-and-ideas/online-resources/how-to-install-and-repair-instrument-binding-and-purfling/how-to-bind-a-fretboard-gibson-style . Newer Gibson binding has a T-section tang [F, Haze].
- Costs: nibs are labour-intensive and "make refretting more difficult" [F] https://www.premierguitar.com/gear/jol-dantzigs-esoterica-electrica-fretboard-treatments-bound-to-please . Keeping them needs "extreme precision"; Haze says "make peace with losing your nibs." Strings can catch: "The first string can easily slip in there when playing and, worse still, it can sometimes snag in there" [F, Haze]. Some say they shorten the usable fret; fret ends can crack binding as wood shrinks [S] https://forum.seymourduncan.com/threads/how-to-deal-with-a-small-gap-between-the-fret-end-and-the-nibs-on-the-binding.253695/ .
- Frets over binding (Martin, Fender per Dantzig): "frets extend over the binding and are beveled", tang removed at each end, frets span the full width, refret "takes a little more effort" [F, Premier Guitar].
- Tang hiding: StewMac cuts "about a 32nd of an inch in from the edge of the binding" with a nipper, smaller bites on stainless [F, StewMac bound-board page]. Frets.com: undercut "as close to the binding as possible"; too far and "the ends have a hard time staying tight" [F] https://mail.frets.com/FretsPages/Luthier/Technique/Frets/FretTrouble/Overhang/overhang.html . Over-removal risks buzzing [F] https://hazeguitars.com/blog/refrets-and-fingerboard-binding .

## 5. Blind slots, tang nipping, filled ends

- Blind-slot inset: a forum poster suggested "1-2 mm" from the edge; opinion only [S] https://groups.google.com/group/obrien-forum/msg/2a226779f009c . A CNC builder notes cutting slots to the board radius and stopping short of the edge [S] https://www.talkbass.com/threads/cnc-fret-board-stuff.1058497/ .
- Filled slot ends: **not found.** Fret-end shaping done before installation requires a blind or filed end; the tang must be nipped (see section 4 tool and "1/32" cut).
- Glue: opinions split (some stopped gluing; one stopped Titebond because string tension seemed to lift frets) [S] https://www.luthiersforum.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=719746 .

## 6. Tangless / glued frets (Parker Fly)

- Parker manual: "hardened stainless steel frets bonded to a glass and carbon fiber fret board"; only the factory may service frets or "fret work done elsewhere voids the warranty"; no adhesive or replacement procedure given [F] https://manualslib.com/manual/306030/Parker-Fly.html?page=5 .
- Hearsay on factory removal ("chisel and whacks the frets off, sands the board smooth, and glues on a new one") [S] unverified. A tech reglued loose frets with glue and clamping and installed a customer-supplied fret [F] https://sites.google.com/thesproutedfret.com/thesproutedfret/on-the-bench/parker-nitefly . FlyClone documents an epoxy jig [S] https://vjmedia.wpi.edu/images/7/72/FlyClone_Fret_to_Fretboard_Jig.pdf . Fret-end shape and sprout behaviour: **not found.** Other tangless systems: **not found.**

## 7. Recessed, inlaid or machine-shaped ends

- Wood shaped up to meet fret ends, recessed or inlaid ends: **not found.**
- CNC-shaped fret ends or pre-shaped wire ends: **not found.** The nearest are Forster's diamond wheel [F], pre-radiused wire (Jescar FW43080-S is sold pre-radiused [S, Philadelphia Luthier Tools]), and a luthier jig that holds the end "a few thousandths inside the fretboard edge" [S] https://www.luthiersforum.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=408374 . ## 8. Rolled fretboard edges

- Methods: sanding block or file after assembly, or machining a radius before or after fretting [F, Premier/Dantzig]. Compression by burnishing: a round bar at 45°, "10 to 15 minutes per edge", "hurrying up the natural process of compressing fibres" [F, Premier "vintage"]. Fine sandpaper with masking [F] https://aeonicfrets.com/blogs/the-bench/rolled-fretboard-edges-worth-it .
- Amount: "A rolled edge is a small radius, not a big chamfer" [F, Aeonic]. One repairer: radius "no more than 1/8"" [S] https://www.acousticguitarforum.com/forums/showthread.php?t=443733 . Heavy roll "will lose up to 1mm" [S, search summary]. Numeric roll widths from named builders: **not found.**
- Interaction with fret ends: a large roll effectively tightens the radius at the ends and makes them harder to seat [S]. Over-rolling "can expose the fret tang or leave the fret ends hanging past the wood" and "the exact recipe for the high E slipping off when you dig in" [F, Aeonic]. Over-bevelling the board makes the fret end "take a dive near the end of the fret"; strings cannot be pulled down "without the string falling off the neck" (Hesh) [F, p=743372]. One poster: "Rolled edges, including rounded fret ends, makes a huge difference in the comfort of a neck" (GuitarDoc), dressing ends "can take a good half hour or so" [F] https://forum.seymourduncan.com/threads/guitar-necks-rolled-edges.286994/page-2 .
- Setback: some recommend "0.010" space between the fret end and the fretboard's edge" while others run ends to the edge [S] https://www.mimf.com/library/Methods_for_rounding_fret_ends_on_a_bound_fretboard-12-31-2011.html .
- Humidity: low humidity shrinks the board, "exposing the metal fret ends"; fix by refiling at low humidity [F, Premier/Dantzig]. StewMac cites 40-50% relative humidity as the dry concern and suggests a case humidifier [F]. Rosewood vs ebony: anecdotes only; ebony said to move more [S] https://www.talkbass.com/threads/does-fret-sprout-sometimes-cure-itself.1161685/ . Stainless vs nickel sprout comparison: **not found.**

## 9. String-to-edge distance and slip-off

- Typical layout: a builder's 1-3/4" nut with "1/8" low E and 3/16" high e from the edge"; "about 1/8" or .120 to .125 per outer string" [S] https://umgf.com/string-spacing-at-the-nut-t117140.html . One player reported "about 1 mm" at the high e (outlier) [S]. No source gives a minimum.
- Maximum push before slipping: **no published measurement or rule found.** Qualitative only: usable fret width matters for players who pull the high E "downward and not have it come off the neck" (Don Ross, cited by Hesh) [F, p=743372]; nibs let the first string slip in and snag [F, Haze]; Warmoth warns of strings slipping off heavily rolled edges [S].

## Most praised for feel

- Frets run to the board edge, small bevel, edge slightly rolled, ends fully polished: Hesh's approach [F] https://luthiersforum.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=743372 .
- Forster's "bullet" ends: "very smooth to the touch", "a fraction more fret real estate" [F] https://nkforsterguitars.com/semi-hemispherical-fret-ends/ .
- Rolled edges with rounded ends on Charvel San Dimas: "Probably the most comfortable neck I have" [F] https://forum.seymourduncan.com/threads/guitar-necks-rolled-edges.286994/page-2 .
- Sadowsky hand-finished rounded ends (second-hand) [S] https://www.talkbass.com/threads/help-sadowsky-me-took-a-dive.1677594/ .
- Frets over binding, bevelled so the neck feels unbound [F, Premier/Dantzig].

## Most criticised

- Full quarter-sphere ends: "gets in the way of the players hand" [S] https://sevenstring.org/threads/how-do-you-feel-about-hemispherical-fret-ends/ ; "just a luthier showing off" [F, p=743372].
- Heavy rolled edges: strings "continually slip off the edge" [S, Warmoth].
- Gibson nibs: string catching and refret cost [F, Haze].
- Over-bevelled ends: string falls off, fret top "dives" [F, p=743372].
- Ibanez treatment inconsistency: ends "snipped" but not rounded [S] https://sevenstring.org/threads/fret-ends-on-new-prestige-guitars.334677/page-2 .
- Sharp factory ends needing owner rework (Warmoth) [S].
